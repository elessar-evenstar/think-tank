Placement help

Position the headband across your forehead with the ear pieces resting behind your ears. Keep hair away from the sensors and make sure the forehead and behind-ear sensors touch your skin. Adjust gently for a snug, comfortable fit.

About this check

Live EEG includes brain activity and signals from eyes, muscles, and movement. Each row uses the same fixed +/-250 microvolt range; larger values are clipped at the edges.

Continue becomes available after all four channels stay within the signal limits and the engagement algorithm accepts fresh data. This checks signal usability, not contact impedance, and does not guarantee artifact-free EEG. Detection thresholds stay unchanged.

Blink Explorer Results

A prompted trial counts as detected when a detection occurs within 1.6 seconds of the cue. Extra detections and detections outside that window count as unexpected.

These counts show how closely the detector responses followed the prompts. They cannot confirm whether you actually blinked each time, and a natural blink between prompts may be counted as unexpected.

Calculation details

Each accepted window contains 512 samples (about two seconds) from AF7 and AF8. Each channel is mean-centered and Hann-windowed before calculating frequency power. Matching powers are then averaged across channels. Values use the existing algorithm's squared-voltage normalization. The display smooths updates; the vertical range is fixed after the first accepted window. Values beyond that range are clipped and reported.

Lab 2: Artifact Explorer

Each colored row shows one EEG channel, or recording location on the headband. Read from left to right to follow four seconds of recording.

Taller waves mean larger changes in recorded voltage. Eyes, muscles, and headband movement can also affect EEG. Both graphs use the same scale so you can compare them fairly.

Red dashed lines mark possible blinks flagged by the software, not confirmed blinks. The Motion row shows how fast the headband is turning, measured by its gyroscope.

Technical details

EEG is shown within +/-600 microvolts of each channel's baseline mean. Motion shows angular speed from 0 to 150 degrees per second. Values outside these ranges are clipped at the graph edges; the graphs do not automatically zoom. Timing is approximate and based on packet arrival. Each movement graph covers the full prompted recording, not automatically detected movement boundaries.

What is ThinkTank?

ThinkTank connects a Muse 2 headband to an interactive aquarium, giving you a way to see its signals affect something on screen. Blink-related electrical changes trigger bubbles, an estimate calculated from EEG frequency bands influences fish speed, and the headband's motion sensors let you adjust the view.

Controls

  • The EEG engagement estimate influences fish speed. Watch the 0-100 speed display as you try maintaining your attention on the aquarium; the number shows how fast the fish are moving within their configured range.

Tank and Lab

Tank turns your Muse signals into aquarium controls: blinks activate bubbles, the EEG engagement estimate influences fish speed, and head movements adjust the view.

Lab lets you look more closely at blink detection, movement artifacts, and EEG frequency bands through three guided activities. The fish keep swimming while sensor-driven controls are paused; switch back to Tank whenever you want to resume.

The Science Behind It

EEG records tiny voltage differences at the scalp that reflect the combined electrical activity of many brain cells. Muse uses electrodes on your forehead and behind your ears to record these signals and send them to ThinkTank.

The recordings also pick up electrical activity from your eyes and muscles, along with changes caused by movement or headset contact. ThinkTank uses some of these signals as inputs and checks for others that could interfere with its EEG estimate.

How Blink Detection Works

A blink can produce a brief downward spike in the recordings behind your ears. ThinkTank looks for a matching change in both channels and compares it with recent background activity to decide whether to count a blink.

A detection activates all five bubble fountains, with a short cooldown to avoid counting the same event repeatedly. Other movements can sometimes trigger the detector too; Blink Explorer lets you see how it responds.

EEG Engagement Estimate

ThinkTank compares the strength of theta, alpha, and beta frequency bands in the forehead recordings to calculate an experimental EEG engagement estimate. The estimate changes gradually and influences how fast the fish swim.

Lab shows the estimate on a 0–100 scale, but this is not a percentage of attention. Tank instead displays Fish speed, from the slowest to the fastest configured speed.

Blinks, muscle activity, and poor headset contact can affect the recording. When usable EEG is unavailable, updates pause and the fish eventually return to their normal speed. Frequency Explorer lets you examine the signals behind the estimate.

How Head Movement Works

Muse tracks head movement with motion sensors, separately from EEG. Turning left zooms out and turning right zooms in, while moving your head up or down changes where the camera looks.

These movements adjust the view gradually rather than copying your head angle directly. Hold still when you reach a view you like, and the camera will settle there.
